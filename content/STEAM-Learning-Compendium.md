# Introduction & Philosophy

## Core Educational Principles

**Learner-Centered Design**: Student agency and exploration drive all learning experiences, with teachers serving as facilitators and co-investigators rather than information deliverers.

**Process Over Product**: While we celebrate beautiful creations, we assess the thinking, iteration, and collaboration that made them possible. Learning happens in the struggle, not just in the success.

**Authentic Problems**: Every project addresses real challenges for real people, connecting classroom learning to community needs and professional practice.

**Collaborative Excellence**: Individual skill building serves collaborative goals, with students learning to leverage diverse strengths for shared impact.

## The Four Cs Framework

**Curiosity**: "I can figure out how to make new things" - Systematic investigation of materials, tools, and methods builds confidence in approaching unfamiliar challenges.

**Confidence**: "I trust the process even when I can't see the end" - Design thinking methodology provides structure for navigating uncertainty and complexity.

**Creativity**: "I generate and combine ideas in new ways" - Constraint-based creativity and rapid iteration unlock innovative solutions within practical boundaries.

**Communication**: "I share thoughts collaboratively with others" - From peer feedback to professional presentation, students build skills for effective knowledge transfer and partnership.

## Assessment Philosophy: Portfolio as Learning Amplification

> **"Learning becomes visible when students capture not just what they made, but how they thought"** - Assessment through authentic documentation and reflective practice.

Traditional testing cannot capture the depth of learning through making, collaborating, and iterating. Our portfolio approach treats assessment as **learning amplification** rather than measurement, helping students see their own growth while building crucial metacognitive skills.

### Core Assessment Principles

**Growth Over Grades**: We assess learning progression rather than point-in-time performance, celebrating intellectual courage and persistence alongside technical achievement.

**Process Over Product**: While we celebrate beautiful creations, we assess the thinking, iteration, and collaboration that made them possible.

**Student Voice Central**: Learners drive assessment through reflection, goal-setting, and evidence curation, developing self-assessment capabilities crucial for independent learning.

**Authentic Documentation**: Assessment artifacts emerge naturally from project work rather than external requirements, making documentation purposeful rather than bureaucratic.

### The Portfolio Ecosystem: Living Documentation

Portfolios grow organically throughout each project, capturing:
- **Process photography** showing iteration and collaboration
- **Design evolution** from initial sketches through final prototypes
- **Problem-solving narratives** describing challenges and breakthrough moments
- **Peer collaboration** evidence and cross-project connections
- **Reflection artifacts** demonstrating metacognitive development

### Reflection Rhythms for Deep Learning

**Daily Captures** (30 seconds): End-of-session photo with one-sentence summary: *"Today I learned..."*

**Weekly Reflections** (5 minutes): Deeper consideration of learning progression: *"This week I got better at... Next week I want to try..."*

**Project Synthesis** (15 minutes): Comprehensive reflection connecting process to bigger learning goals: *"This project helped me understand... The most challenging part was... I'm proud of how I..."*

**Semester Integration** (30 minutes): Cross-project pattern recognition and goal-setting: *"Looking across my projects, I notice... My biggest growth was in... I'm excited to apply these skills to..."*

# The Learning Journey

## Days 1-3: Foundation Building Through Design Thinking

### Day 1: First Steps in STEAM
> **"Curiosity, Confidence, Creativity, Communication"** - The four principles that guide our semester journey.

**The Connected Words Design Challenge**: Create four connected words using **only cardstock and scissors**:
- Each word must be **one solid piece** (no breaks in letters)
- Words must be **readable from across the room** (scale considerations)
- Use **only three pieces of cardstock total** for the team
- Maintain **similar style** across all four words

**Design Thinking in Action**:

**Empathize**: Understanding your teammates - Who are your collaborators and what are their strengths? How do different people approach design problems?

**Define**: The real constraints - Physical constraints (cardstock strength, cutting precision), team constraints (time pressure, different skill levels), functional constraints (readability, structural integrity)

**Ideate**: Individual then collaborative - *"No talking yet, on your own. This is always the best for creativity."* Silent brainstorming maximizes creative potential before group collaboration.

**Prototype**: Rapid construction with division of labor and collaborative making, testing connections as development progresses.

**Test**: Real-time assessment - *"Do we have four solid words that work together?"*

## The 4Ms Framework: Systems Thinking for Makers

> **"Maker, Machine, Method, Materials, Margin"** - A systematic approach to understanding and planning any making project based on the Ishikawa diagram for root cause analysis, adapted for educational making.

Every making project involves complex interactions between multiple factors. The 4 Ms framework helps makers think systematically about all elements contributing to project success or failure.

```
    MAKER ← [skills, experience, goals]
       ↓
PROJECT SUCCESS
       ↓  
  MACHINE ← [tools, capabilities, limitations]
       ↓
   METHOD ← [processes, techniques, workflows]  
       ↓
 MATERIALS ← [properties, cost, availability]
       ↓
   MARGIN ← [time, materials, error tolerance]
```
