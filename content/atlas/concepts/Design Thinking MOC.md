> [!tldr] TL;DR
> Design Thinking is our core process for solving problems. It's a five-step map: **Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test**. We use it for everything, from making [[efforts/Family Coasters|gifts for family]] to building [[efforts/Robot Storage|tools for teachers]].

> [!quote] Why it matters
> This process stops you from jumping to solutions before you understand the real problem. It encourages creativity, reduces the fear of failure, and leads to solutions that people actually want and need. Mastering this is more valuable than mastering any single tool.

---

## Key Ideas: The Five Phases

Design Thinking is not a strict, linear process. It's a flexible cycle where you might loop back and repeat steps as you learn.

1.  **[[atlas/concepts/Design Thinking#empathize|1. Empathize]]**: Understand the people you are designing for.
    -   **Key practice**: [[atlas/concepts/Observation Sketching|Observation & Sketching]]
    -   **In action**: For the [[efforts/Robot Storage|Robot Storage]] project, we interviewed and observed the physics teacher to understand her daily workflow and frustrations.

2.  **[[atlas/concepts/Design Thinking#define|2. Define]]**: Clearly articulate the problem you are trying to solve.
    -   **Key practice**: Reframing challenges as "How Might We..." questions.
    -   **In action**: The problem evolved from "store robots" to "optimize the daily robot workflow to maximize learning time."

3.  **[[atlas/concepts/Design Thinking#ideate|3. Ideate]]**: Brainstorm a wide range of creative solutions.
    -   **Key practice**: [[atlas/concepts/Brainstorming Methods|Collaborative Brainstorming]] and using [[atlas/concepts/AI Integration|AI as a creative partner]].
    -   **In action**: We used Gemini to visualize different storage concepts for the robots before building anything.

4.  **[[atlas/concepts/Design Thinking#prototype|4. Prototype]]**: Build a quick, low-cost version of your idea to test it.
    -   **Key practice**: [[atlas/concepts/Cardboard Prototyping|Cardboard Prototyping]] to test size and function.
    -   **In action**: We built cardboard mockups of the robot holders to check dimensions before using expensive plywood.

5.  **[[atlas/concepts/Design Thinking#test|5. Test]]**: Get feedback on your prototype from real users.
    -   **Key practice**: Gathering [[atlas/concepts/User Feedback|User Feedback]] and practicing [[atlas/concepts/Iterative Refinement|Iterative Refinement]].
    -   **In action**: The teacher tested our prototype and we discovered the slot for the USB cable was too small, a critical insight that saved the project.
