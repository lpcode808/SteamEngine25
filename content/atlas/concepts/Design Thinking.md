# Design Thinking: A Problem-Solving Framework

> **"Understand problems before jumping to solutions"** - The mindset that guides all STEAM learning.

---

## What is Design Thinking?

Design thinking is a human-centered approach to innovation that integrates the needs of people, the possibilities of technology, and requirements for business success. In our STEAM course, it became the foundation for everything from [[efforts/Family Coasters|personal coasters]] to [[efforts/Robot Storage|professional teacher solutions]].

### The Stanford d.school Process

**[[#empathize|Empathize]]** → **[[#define|Define]]** → **[[#ideate|Ideate]]** → **[[#prototype|Prototype]]** → **[[#test|Test]]**

*But remember: This isn't a linear path! Real design thinking loops back, skips steps, and iterates constantly.*

## Define {#define}
### **Framing the Right Problem**

> *"Constraint as creative catalyst, not limitation"*

**In Practice**: [Day 19](../calendar/consolidated.md#day-19)

Students refined problem-and-solution pairs while learning to articulate challenges clearly and specifically, avoiding jumping to solutions too quickly.

#### Problem Definition Strategies
- **How Might We...?** questions that open possibilities
- **[[atlas/concepts/4Ms Framework|5 Whys Technique]]**: Digging deeper into root causes  
- **Constraint Identification**: Real limitations vs. assumed ones

## Test {#test}
### **Learning from Reality**

> *"Prototype iteration and learning the various techniques"*

**In Practice**: [Day 31](../calendar/consolidated.md#day-31)

#### Testing Strategies
- **[[atlas/concepts/User Feedback|Real User Feedback]]**: Testing with actual teachers and classmates
- **[[atlas/concepts/Functional Testing|Functional Testing]]**: Does it solve the problem it's meant to solve?
- **[[atlas/concepts/Iterative Refinement|Iterative Refinement]]**: Small improvements based on testing results

## Design Thinking in STEAM Context

### Integration with [[atlas/concepts/4Ms Framework|The 4 Ms]]
- **Maker**: The human-centered focus of design thinking
- **Machine**: Choosing tools based on prototyping needs
- **Method**: Design thinking as overarching methodology  
- **Materials**: Testing material choices through iteration
- **Margin**: Building in room for iteration and improvement

### Collaboration with [[atlas/concepts/AI Integration|AI Tools]]
- **Empathize**: AI for user research and insight synthesis
- **Define**: AI for problem reframing and question generation
- **Ideate**: AI as brainstorming partner and possibility expander
- **Prototype**: AI for rapid concept visualization and code generation
- **Test**: AI for feedback analysis and improvement suggestions

## Applying Design Thinking

### In Your Own Projects
1. **Start with Empathy**: Who are you designing for? What do they really need?
2. **Define Clearly**: What problem are you actually solving?
3. **Ideate Widely**: Generate many possibilities before committing to one
4. **Prototype Quickly**: Test ideas with simple, fast models
5. **Test Authentically**: Get feedback from real users in real contexts

### Beyond the Classroom
- **[[atlas/connections/Cross-Curricular|Academic Projects]]**: Apply to research papers, science experiments, art projects
- **[[atlas/connections/Community Engagement|Community Challenges]]**: Address local problems with design thinking
